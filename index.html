Research Interests

My research interests lie in representation and context for robot learning. I study what information robots need, how that information should be represented, and why particular representations make robot learning more effective.

My recent work explores these questions from two perspectives: disentangling task context, such as terrain and robot-specific properties, and structuring temporal context for sequential prediction. In the latter, I study how predictions can better reflect sequential dependencies while remaining revisable across the horizon, allowing earlier and later predictions to influence and correct one another in a more human-like iterative manner.

Going forward, I want to develop robot learning methods that can identify, represent, and effectively use the right context for each task. I am particularly interested in in-context learning, discovering useful representations and inductive structures, and designing learning mechanisms that enable efficient adaptation and generalization. At the same time, I want to better understand why particular contexts and representations are effective, and connect these empirical successes to more principled and mathematically grounded explanations.

I have experience across diverse robotic domains, including quadruped locomotion and policy learning, robotic manipulation, autonomous navigation, null-space control, and autonomous driving. Working across these different systems has shaped my interest in finding representations and learning principles that remain useful across different embodiments, tasks, and environments.
